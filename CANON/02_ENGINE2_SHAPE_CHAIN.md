## 1 · THE SHAPE TAXONOMY

Every session exhibits one of these, or a variant. Shapes are defined mechanically by the
relationship between price action and the E1 zone architecture — never by eyeballing.

| Shape | Definition | E1 signature |
|---|---|---|
| **SS — Swing Scalps** | Price oscillates inside a range bounded by zones above (ceiling) and below (floor); swings respect the boundaries. Each swing 1–3 sessions. | **Both** bounding zones **ZFP < 30%**. Expiry positioning cages price between large strikes. |
| **BS — Breakout Surge** | Price breaks decisively through a zone, then surges directionally to the next zone / Structural Extreme Magnet. Confirmed by sustained move beyond the zone (**15+ min**). | Failing zone **ZFP > 45%** with active breakout pre-conditions. |
| **R — Recovery** | Temporary stabilisation / mild retracement immediately after a BS or WL. **Always transitional.** | Mid-range zone, **ZFP 20–40%**, typically a Post-Expiry Ghost. |
| **WL — Whiplash** | Rapid violent move that immediately reverses — spike-and-reject. **The characteristic is the rejection at the zone, not the initial surge.** | Rejection zone **ZFP < 20%**, confluence ≥ 8, LSZ present. |
| **WLS — Whiplash Storm** | Extended sequence of explosive whipsaws across sessions. On 4H it *looks like* SS with dramatically increased volatility. **Almost always resolves into a BS.** | 3+ zones of similar degree inside a narrow band; price oscillating violently. |

**Variants:**
- **PBS — Piercing BS**: a BS so powerful it pierces intermediate zones without pausing
  (failing zone ZFP > 70%).
- **RWL — Reverse Whiplash**: a WL in the *opposite* direction of the preceding BS. **The most
  common follow-on shape after a BS.**
- **PWL — Piercing WL**: the surge pierces multiple zones before rejection.
- **PRWL — Piercing Reverse Whiplash**: RWL with piercing characteristics. **The strongest
  reversal signal in the taxonomy.**
- **RS — Recovery Surge**: instead of slow consolidation, Recovery takes the form of a sharp
  directional move back toward the pre-breakout level.
- **SS(DWL) — Double Whiplash**: two rapid whipsaws inside the range — heightened volatility,
  often preceding a breakout attempt.

## 2 · SHAPE CLASSIFICATION DECISION MATRIX

Apply **in sequence from priority 1**; the first match wins.

| # | Condition (E1 metrics) | Shape | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Price breaks through a zone with **ZFP > 45%** AND confirms 15+ min; Measured Move target exists | **BS** (or **PBS** if it pierces multiple) | HIGH |
| 2 | Price surges to a zone with **ZFP < 20%**, briefly pierces, then rejects; sweep target identified | **WL** (or **PWL**) | HIGH |
| 3 | After BS/WL: price stabilises at a mid-range zone (**ZFP 20–40%**); duration < 1 session | **R** (or **RS** if sharp) | MODERATE |
| 4 | **3+ zones** in a narrow range (0.30–0.50 DXY); violent oscillation; 4H shows consolidation | **WLS** (or Minor WLS if 1–2 sessions) | HIGH |
| 5 | Oscillating between Heavy/Extreme ceiling & floor, **both ZFP < 30%**, duration > 1 session | **SS** | HIGH |
| 6 | After a BS: violent reversal in the opposite direction through the prior origin; Hard Wall rejection | **RWL** (or **PRWL** if piercing) | MODERATE-HIGH |

## 5 · ★ ZFP-CONDITIONED TRANSITIONS — THE MOST IMPORTANT E2 RULE

Chain sequencing must be conditioned on the **TARGET zone's ZFP**, not on the momentum of the
current move. Without this table the engine predicts R or PBS after a BS reaches a Hard Wall,
when it must predict **RWL**.

| Current | Target zone ZFP | Predicted next | Confidence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BS | **< 20% (Hard Wall)** | **RWL** — rejection, NOT R/PBS | HIGH | the zone is a Hard Wall — price WILL be rejected. **Do not predict continuation.** |
| BS | 20–40% (mid-range) | **R** — stabilisation | HIGH | moderate strength — pause, not necessarily hard reject |
| BS | > 60% (very weak) | **PBS** — cascade continues | MODERATE | the zone is failing |
| BS | > 80% (nearly broken) | **PBS** — strong cascade | HIGH | effectively broken — continuation with momentum |
| SS | > 45% (bounding zone failing) | **BS** — breakout imminent | HIGH | one side of the range is failing |
| SS | all < 30% (both hold) | **SS continues** | HIGH | both boundaries solid |
| WLS | any (bounding zone fails) | **BS** | VERY HIGH | WLS almost always resolves into BS — only the direction is in question |
| R | persists > 1 full session | **SS** — *reclassify*, not continue R | MODERATE-HIGH | if stabilisation persists it IS consolidation now |
| R | < 1 session, opposing zone near | **WL or RWL** | MODERATE | brief pause then rejection |
| WL | rejection momentum builds opposite | **RWL** | MODERATE-HIGH | the rejection is the entry for the reverse move |
| WL | no opposite momentum | **SS** — consolidation | MODERATE | whiplash exhausts into a range |

> **CRITICAL MISTAKE TO AVOID.** When a BS reaches a Hard Wall (ZFP < 20%, confluence ≥ 8), the
> engine **MUST** predict **RWL**, not R or PBS. This was the **#1 E2 error** that caused the
> Apr 20–24 misprediction — the engine predicted upside continuation with a Hard Wall sitting
> directly above. **The ZFP of the TARGET zone determines the next shape — not the momentum of
> the current move.**

**This is exactly the rule the live build inverts** by substituting the confluence-band ZFP for
the geometric ZFP (CANON/01 §7).

## 8 · THE FORWARD MAPPING PROCEDURE (mandatory, 5 steps)

**Step 1 — Current shape.** Apply the Decision Matrix (§2) to the current price-zone
relationship. State the shape, the anchoring zone, and that zone's ZFP.
**Step 2 — Transition probability.** Apply §4 + §5. If the current shape is SS, also assess the
consolidation phase (§10).
**Step 3 — Chain extension with audit trail.** Extend shape by shape, documenting for each link:
(a) current shape, (b) the zone trigger, (c) the ZFP condition, (d) the predicted next shape,
(e) confidence. **Max chain length 5. Terminate if cumulative confidence < 25%.**
Format: `Shape A → [Trigger: zone, ZFP X%] → Shape B → […] → Shape C [cumulative: Y%]`
**Step 4 — Alternative chain (mandatory).** For every primary chain build ≥1 alternative that
activates if the primary fails at a **Decision Zone**. Document: the Decision Zone, the ZFP
threshold that flips it, the alternative chain, its confidence.
Format: `PRIMARY: A→B→C | DECISION ZONE: [zone] ZFP > [t]% → ALTERNATIVE: A→X→Y`
**Step 5 — Session/day-level decomposition.** For each day Mon–Fri: predicted shape, directional
bias (up/down/range), the key zone, volatility triggers, confidence.
Format: `Mon: [SHAPE] [dir] | Key Zone: [name] | Triggers: [list] | Confidence: X%`

**Pair translation of the chain:** the **shapes are identical**, only the **direction inverts**
(DXY up = pair down). SS stays SS; BS stays BS. This is the directional-conviction principle
(CANON/06).

## 11 · SUPPORTING E2 MECHANICS

**4H MoM one-and-done.** A 4H MoM that is 100% unmitigated *will* react on the first retest, but
often only minorly, then a strong pierce. Expected shape after touching a 4H MoM = **minor WL
followed by BS/PBS through the zone**. Never a swing entry.

**Timeframe element matching (TP targeting).** Each timeframe's liquidity is its own element;
moves originating from a TF's zone target the *same* TF's zone on the other side:
4H→4H (1H acceptable, 4H dominant) · 1H→1H (30m ok) · 30m→30m (15m ok) · 15m→30m/1H (max 2 up)
· 5m→15m/30m (max 2 up). **A 15m-originated breakout should NOT target a 4H zone** — it will be
absorbed first. Targeting across more than 2 dimensions is a category error.

**Pressure reading (directional bias when consolidation is unclear):**
- **Candle size = pressure direction.** The obviously bigger candles show where pressure builds.
- **Wick direction = pressure direction.** Upper wicks = buying pressure; lower wicks = selling
  pressure.
- **Triangle shapes:** the **flat** side is *receiving* pressure; the **sharp** side is
  *applying* pressure.
- **4H storyline tiebreaker:** when in doubt, the 4H storyline bias decides. The weekly trend is
  identified on the 4H and takes days/weeks to complete.

**Trading speed rule.** Assume price moves at **30–50%** of the expected speed.
(a) Add **1 day** to expected timing — today's move often becomes tomorrow's.
(b) A 4H breakout takes ~2× as long as it looks.
(c) **Space reinforcement rockets further apart** — too close and they all trigger at once (fine
   for position building, bad for risk if the move stalls).
(d) AUD/USD: 2–4 days per HTF move.
(e) Consolidation lasts 2–4 days.
